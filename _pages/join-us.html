What you would work on

Students in the lab contribute to numerical modeling, code development, data analysis, visualization and validation studies, with applications in energy, aerospace, propulsion, disperse multiphase flows, mixing and reactive flows, environmental flows and industrial systems.

Current CSULB students should first look through our research areas and recent publications. This helps identify topics that match your interests, background and long-term goals.

Opportunities

Depending on project needs and advising capacity, opportunities may include:

  • Bachelor's thesis projects
  • Master's thesis projects
  • Undergraduate research projects
  • Independent study
  • Volunteer research
  • Funded research assistant positions
  • Project-based collaborations

What we look for

Successful students in the lab are curious, self-motivated, reliable and willing to learn. Experience with CFD, programming, fluid mechanics, thermodynamics, numerical methods or data analysis helps, but it is not required for every project.

Students should be ready to communicate regularly, document their work clearly, keep research files organized, and work carefully through technical challenges.

Prospective students

Prospective students should first apply to CSULB through the official admissions process. Admission decisions are made by the university, and the lab cannot help with admission processing or application review. After admission, you are welcome to contact Prof. Madadi following the guidelines on this page.
